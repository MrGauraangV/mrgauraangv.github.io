A little about me

Hi there!

Gauraang Varshney · IIT Roorkee

I'm a Master's student at IIT Roorkee, with a keen interest in astrophysical phenomena through computation. At the moment, the problem of planet formation appeals to me. It is not completely a mystery, but the theoretical models behind protoplanetary discs — the construction zones for planetary systems — make use of all kinds of physics.

In general, I like falling down the rabbit hole of chaotic problems and digging through them for a messy, yet elegant, underlying solution that computational techniques may reveal.

My current focus is on applying fluid dynamics to model the evolution of dust within protoplanetary disc structures, particularly the role of turbulence in the growth, settling and concentration of solids on their way towards planetesimals.

Prior to this work, I explored variable stars through observational techniques at the Aryabhatta Research Institute of Observational Sciences (ARIES). This involved analysing photometric data and building pipelines for period detection. Although interesting while it lasted, the experience made me realise that I prefer the theoretical and computational side of astronomy much more than the data-analysis side.

Current research

JANUARY 2026 — PRESENT   /   M.SC. THESIS

Dust growth and vertical settling in protoplanetary discs

Supervisor: Dr. Akshay Bhatnagar · Indian Institute of Technology Roorkee

I'm investigating the vertical evolution of dust in protoplanetary discs, focusing on the interplay between aerodynamic coupling, gravitational settling, turbulent diffusion and particle growth.

So far, I've derived analytical solutions for dust growth during laminar vertical settling and for steady-state dust distributions under competing settling and turbulent diffusion. I've also numerically integrated particle trajectories and grain-growth equations using Epstein drag, and compared the results with the analytical predictions. One aspect I'm exploring is how the height dependence of the Epstein stopping time changes the vertical concentration of dust.

I'm now extending this work into a vertically resolved, multi-species dust-coagulation framework, with the aim of studying grain growth under turbulent relative velocities and gas dynamics.

Some analytical and numerical results from this project: grain growth in a laminar model, dust-density profiles with diffusion, and settling trajectories. Figures reproduced from my research portfolio.

Previous work & projects

DEC 2025 — FEB 2026

Variable stars in NGC 2506 · ARIES

At ARIES, under Dr. Yogesh Chandra Joshi, I worked with multi-epoch optical observations from the 3.6-m Devasthal Optical Telescope. I developed a differential-photometry pipeline and used Lomb–Scargle and Box Least Squares methods to search for periodic variability, followed by cross-matching with TESS and Gaia catalogues.

JUN — JUL 2025

Cosmological parameters from Type Ia supernovae

At India Space Academy, I analysed the Pantheon Type Ia supernova dataset using numerical fitting and residual analysis to study cosmological parameters and the age of the Universe. My ΛCDM fit gave H₀ = 72.97 ± 0.26 km s⁻¹ Mpc⁻¹ and Ωₘ = 0.351 ± 0.019 in that analysis.
